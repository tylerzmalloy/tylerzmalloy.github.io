FIG.01 — PRODUCT / UX

Chessly — Homepage & Platform Redesign

SHIPPED
& LOVED
IT
Goal

Design a homepage and platform that increases subscription conversion and gamifies chess learning to improve retention.

Problem

The homepage failed to communicate Chessly's core value — learning from GothamChess, the world's largest chess YouTuber. The site felt insider-focused, confusing, and hard to navigate.

Approach

Sketched a full layout redesign leading with a hero section featuring Levy and a clear value proposition. Partnered with the Chessly team to identify conversion-driving content — reviews, average ELO increase, time in platform — then built working mockups in Figma.

Solution

A modern, conversion-focused homepage plus updated platform UX, including gamified elements: points, puzzles, and a leaderboard.

Chessly's user base grew from ~450K to 1.3M following implementation. As a contract designer, Tyler wasn't part of post-launch measurement — this is framed as strong directional evidence, backed by the before/after design work itself.
→ still my favorite redesign
FIG.02 — LEADERSHIP / ENROLLMENT

Greenville University / Core Education — Creative Leadership & Application Redesign

Application flow — form + progress bar
Progress-bar detail as the form nears completion
Goal

Rebuild how marketing functioned at Greenville University — from a reactive request-filler into a real strategic function — while directly supporting enrollment and fundraising during a period of financial pressure.

Problem

The university's brand was inconsistently represented across poor-quality materials, with no clear "GU story." Marketing operated as an internal job shop rather than a strategic team. The application process itself — the most critical conversion point — was clunky, overly long, and actively losing prospective students.

Approach

As creative lead, restructured how the creative team operates — directing a team across content, copy, and campaigns — while personally owning web, high-priority video, and campaign design. Took direct ownership of the application redesign: simplifying fields, removing friction, adding progress indicators and smart fields, and implementing tracking that hadn't existed before.

Solution

A modernized application experience and a more strategically-run creative function overall — including leading all creative for the university's biannual "Big Give" campaign.

Application completion rose from 6% to 9.4% — a substantial lift for a university application. Creative and marketing leadership contributed to the two highest incoming classes in school history, and directly supported fundraising, including $119K raised in a single Big Give campaign.
↑ one progress bar did that
FIG.03 — WEB / BRAND / VIDEO

Pocket Testament League — Web Redesign & National Video Campaign

Early UX critique notes — donation & checkout flow
Goal

Modernize Pocket Testament League's digital presence end-to-end and produce a flagship video to drive mission awareness and giving.

Problem

PTL's website, UX/UI, and campaign creative all lagged well behind what a modern nonprofit needed — unstructured, dated, and not built around how donors actually decide to give.

Approach

Conducted market research into nonprofit sites that successfully balanced mission storytelling with strong UX/UI, and found a consistent pattern: donors want to see their impact, not just read about it. Applied that insight directly — including a homepage graph visualizing giving growth year over year — and designed individual giving pages for each world region PTL serves, alongside a general giving page, to make the ask more specific and personal.

Solution

A full site redesign (structure, UX/UI, giving pages) plus a $10K+ national video — an emotionally-driven mission film designed to make viewers feel part of the cause.

54% increase in revenue following the redesign. The video became a staple asset in PTL's ongoing mission updates and campaigns, directly influencing giving, and was praised by leadership for both production quality and results.
